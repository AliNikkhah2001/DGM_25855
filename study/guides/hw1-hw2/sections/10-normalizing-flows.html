Normalizing flows

Change of variables as a model

Choose a simple base \(u\sim p_0=\mathcal{N}(0,I)\) and a stack of invertible maps \(y=f(u)\). Equation gives the exact density of \(y\). There is no ELBO. Training maximizes the log-density of the data, which is a base log-density plus a sum of log-absolute-determinants, one per layer. Lecture 5, slides 3–34, builds this from equivalent events up to the chain rule for Jacobians: \[\log|\det J_{f_K\circ\cdots\circ f_1}| =\sum_{k=1}^K\log|\det J_{f_k}|.\]

Intuition

A flow spends its capacity on maps whose Jacobian determinant is cheap. A generic neural network is invertible only by accident, and its determinant costs \(O(D^3)\). Coupling layers, autoregressive maps, and \(1\times 1\) convolutions are the designs that keep the determinant in closed form.

Affine coupling

Split \(h=(h_A,h_B)\) with dimensions \(d_A+d_B=D\). RealNVP, Lecture 5 slides 52–64, sets \[\begin{aligned} y_A&=h_A,\\ y_B&=h_B\odot\exp\left(s(h_A)\right)+t(h_A). \end{aligned}\] The functions \(s\) and \(t\) are ordinary neural networks. They need not be invertible.

Result: Homework 2, problem 4.1

The map is invertible: \[\begin{aligned} h_A&=y_A,\\ h_B&=\left(y_B-t(y_A)\right)\odot\exp\left(-s(y_A)\right). \end{aligned}\] Order the coordinates as \((A,B)\). The Jacobian is block triangular, \[J=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\ \ast& \operatorname{diag}(e^{s(h_A)})\end{pmatrix},\] so \(\log|\det J|=\sum_{j=1}^{d_B}s_j(h_A)\). Therefore \[\log p(y)=\log p_0(f^{-1}(y))-\sum_{j=1}^{d_B}s_j(y_A).\] The networks \(s\) and \(t\) do not appear in the determinant. Only the scale values do. That is why a large network is allowed.

NICE is the special case \(s\equiv 0\), an additive coupling. Then \(\det J=1\). A separate diagonal rescaling layer carries the change of volume. Alternating which half is updated, and inserting a permutation of channels between couplings, lets every coordinate be transformed.

Practice: Invert a \(2\)-D coupling by hand

Let \(s(h_1)=\log 2\) and \(t(h_1)=h_1\). Forward-map \((h_1,h_2)=(1,3)\). Then invert the result.

Worked solution

\(y_1=1\) and \(y_2=3\cdot e^{\log 2}+1=7\). The inverse reads \(h_1=y_1=1\) and \(h_2=(7-1)\cdot e^{-\log 2}=3\). The log-absolute-determinant of the forward map is \(\log 2\).

A flow as a VAE prior

Keep a decoder \(p_\theta(x\mid z)\) and replace the prior by a flow. Draw \(u\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\), set \(z=f_\psi(u)\), and let \(p_\psi\) be the induced density of \(z\). The ELBO is still reconstruction minus a KL, \[\mathcal L_{\theta,\psi}(x) =\mathbb{E}_{q(z\mid x)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(q(z\mid x)\| p_\psi(z)\right),\] and the change of variables writes the prior log-density as \[\log p_\psi(z)=\log p_0(f^{-1}(z))-\log|\det J_f(f^{-1}(z))|.\] Equivalently, define a decoder on the base space by \(\tilde p(x\mid u)=p(x\mid f(u))\). Integrating \(u\) against \(p_0\) gives the same marginal \(p(x)\) as integrating \(z\) against \(p_\psi\). If \(z\sim q(z\mid x)\) and \(u=f^{-1}(z)\), the induced density is \[r(u\mid x)=q(f(u)\mid x) |\det J_f(u)|,\] and \(\mathrm{KL}(q\| p_\psi)=\mathrm{KL}(r\| p_0)\). The two ELBOs agree when the variational densities are exact images of each other.

Watch out: Where the equivalence dies

Restrict both variational families to diagonal Gaussians. Take \(f(u_1,u_2)=(u_1,u_2+u_1^2)\) and \(q(z)=\mathcal{N}(0,I_2)\). Section Matrix calculus and Jacobians showed that \(\det J_f=1\) and that the induced density of \(u=f^{-1}(z)\) is \[r(u)\propto\exp\left(-\frac12 u_1^2-\frac12(u_2+u_1^2)^2\right),\] up to the constant of the standard normal. This is not a diagonal Gaussian: the coordinates are dependent. No diagonal-Gaussian encoder in \(u\)-space represents \(r\). Optimizing over diagonal Gaussians in \(z\) and in \(u\) are different problems, even though the generative models are the same.

Masked autoregressive flows

An autoregressive map with \(y_d=\mu_d(y_{<d})+\sigma_d(y_{<d}) \varepsilon_d\) is triangular. Its log-determinant is \(\sum_d\log\sigma_d\). MADE masks evaluate every \(\mu_d\) and \(\sigma_d\) in one pass. Likelihood is parallel. Sampling remains sequential, because \(y_d\) needs \(y_{<d}\). That is Lecture 5, slides 65–73, and it is part III of the flow notebook: a two-dimensional MAF on Lab chroma. An inverse autoregressive flow swaps the cost. Sampling becomes parallel and likelihood becomes sequential. The notebook asks for a MAF, not an IAF.

From a density to a pixel probability

A flow defines a density on \(\mathbb{R}^D\). An image \(x\in\mathbb{Z}^D\) is discrete. Assign to \(x\) the bin \(B_x=x+[0,1)^D\). These bins partition \(\mathbb{R}^D\) up to boundaries of measure zero. If \(y\) has density \(p_\theta\) and \(x=\lfloor y\rfloor\), then \[P_\theta(x)=\int_{[0,1)^D}p_\theta(x+u) du.\] Each value is nonnegative, and the sum over \(x\in\mathbb{Z}^D\) equals \(\int_{\mathbb{R}^D}p_\theta=1\). So \(P_\theta\) is a probability mass function. The number \(p_\theta(x)\) is a density evaluated at the corner of the bin. It is not \(P_\theta(x)\).

The integral has no closed form for a neural flow. Choose a dequantizer \(q(u\mid x)\) supported on the unit bin, strictly positive inside it. Importance sampling writes \[P_\theta(x)=\mathbb{E}_{q(u\mid x)}\left[\frac{p_\theta(x+u)}{q(u\mid x)}\right].\] Jensen produces the variational dequantization bound \[ \log P_\theta(x) \ge \mathbb{E}_{q}\left[\log\frac{p_\theta(x+u)}{q(u\mid x)}\right] =\mathbb{E}_q[\log p_\theta(x+u)]+H\left(q(\cdot\mid x)\right).\] The first term wants \(x+u\) where the flow density is high. The entropy stops \(q\) from collapsing to a point, which would throw away the integral.

Define the normalized restriction of the model to the bin, \[r_\theta(u\mid x)=\frac{p_\theta(x+u)}{P_\theta(x)},\qquad u\in[0,1)^D.\] The same KL algebra as gives \[\log P_\theta(x)-\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DQ}} =\mathrm{KL}\left(q(u\mid x)\| r_\theta(u\mid x)\right).\] The bound is tight if and only if \(q=r_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\).

Uniform dequantization sets \(q=1\) on the bin. The bound becomes \(\int_{[0,1)^D}\log p_\theta(x+u) du\), and it is tight if and only if \(p_\theta(x+u)\) is constant on the bin. A conditional flow \(u=g_\phi(\varepsilon;x)\), with \(\varepsilon\sim p_0\) and \(g_\phi(\cdot;x)\) a bijection onto \((0,1)^D\), substitutes the change-of-variables formula into : \[\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DQ}} =\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}\left[ \log p_\theta\left(x+g_\phi(\varepsilon;x)\right) +\log|\det J_{g}(\varepsilon;x)| -\log p_0(\varepsilon) \right].\] A learned \(g\) can match a non-uniform \(r\), so the KL gap can be smaller than the uniform gap.

The probability of an integer is the area of the density over the unit bin, not the height of the density.

Invertible \(1\times 1\) convolutions

RealNVP mixes channels with a fixed permutation. Glow learns the mix. For a tensor \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times H\times W}\), \[Y_{:,i,j}=W X_{:,i,j},\qquad W\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times C},\] with the same \(W\) at every spatial site.

Result: Homework 2, problem 5

The map is invertible if and only if \(\det W\neq 0\), and the inverse applies \(W^{-1}\) at each site. Vectorize the tensor in an order that keeps the \(C\) channels of each pixel together. The full Jacobian is block diagonal with \(HW\) blocks, each equal to \(W\). Hence \[\log|\det J|=HW\log|\det W|.\] With the parameterization \(W=PL\left(U+\operatorname{diag}(s)\right)\) of the previous determinant lemma, \[\log|\det J|=HW\sum_{i=1}^{C}\log| s_i|.\] The cost of the log-determinant is linear in the number of channels, not cubic, and it does not grow with a matrix factorization at every pixel.

Practice: One pixel, two channels

Let \(H=W=1\) and \(W=\operatorname{diag}(2,1/2)\). Compute \(\log|\det J|\) two ways: from \(HW\log|\det W|\), and from the sum of \(\log| s_i|\).

Worked solution

\(\det W=1\), so \(\log|\det J|=0\). And \(\log| 2|+\log| 1/2|=\log 2+\log(1/2)=0\). The volume of the two channels is unchanged: one axis stretches by \(2\), the other shrinks by \(2\).

Homework question: Homework 2, problem 3.1

Consider a VAE with flow prior \(u\sim p_0=\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)\), \(z=f_\psi(u)\), and decoder \(x\sim p_\theta(x\mid z)\). Assume \(f_\psi\) and \(f_\psi^{-1}\) are \(C^1\) with nonsingular Jacobians. Using change of variables, derive the ELBO and show that \[\mathcal L_{\theta,\psi}(x) =\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z\mid x)\| p_\psi(z)\right),\] where \[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z\mid x)\| p_\psi(z)\right) &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}\left[ \log q_\phi(z\mid x) -\log p_0\left(f_\psi^{-1}(z)\right)\\ &\qquad+\log|\det J_{f_\psi}\left(f_\psi^{-1}(z)\right)| \right]. \end{aligned}\]

Homework answer: Homework 2, problem 3.1

The ordinary VAE identity reconstruction minus KL still holds with prior \(p_\psi\). Change of variables gives \[\log p_\psi(z) =\log p_0\left(f_\psi^{-1}(z)\right) -\log|\det J_{f_\psi}\left(f_\psi^{-1}(z)\right)|.\] Therefore \[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi\| p_\psi) &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\left[\log q_\phi(z\mid x)-\log p_\psi(z)\right]\\ &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\left[ \log q_\phi(z\mid x) -\log p_0(f_\psi^{-1}(z)) +\log|\det J_{f_\psi}(f_\psi^{-1}(z))| \right], \end{aligned}\] and \[\mathcal L_{\theta,\psi}(x) =\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)]-\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi\| p_\psi).\]

Homework question: Homework 2, problem 3.2

Define a model with standard Gaussian prior \(p_0(u)\) and decoder \(\tilde p_{\theta,\psi}(x\mid u)=p_\theta(x\mid f_\psi(u))\). Prove that \[p_{\theta,\psi}(x)=\int p_\theta(x\mid f_\psi(u)) p_0(u) du.\] For \(z\sim q_\phi(z\mid x)\) set \(u=f_\psi^{-1}(z)\). Derive \[r_{\phi,\psi}(u\mid x)=q_\phi\left(f_\psi(u)\mid x\right) |\det J_{f_\psi}(u)|,\] and prove \(\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi\| p_\psi)=\mathrm{KL}(r_{\phi,\psi}\| p_0)\). Hence show the two ELBOs agree when the variational densities are related by this map.

Now restrict both variational families to diagonal Gaussians. Take \(f(u_1,u_2)=(u_1,u_2+u_1^2)\) and \(q(z\mid x)=\mathcal{N}(0,I_2)\). Find the induced density \(r(u\mid x)\) and prove it is not diagonal Gaussian. Explain why ELBO optimization can break the equivalence under this restriction even though the generative models agree.

Homework answer: Homework 2, problem 3.2

Change variables \(z=f_\psi(u)\) in the marginal: \(dz=|\det J_{f_\psi}(u)| du\) and \(p_\psi(z) dz=p_0(u) du\), so \[p_{\theta,\psi}(x) =\int p_\theta(x\mid z) p_\psi(z) dz =\int p_\theta(x\mid f_\psi(u)) p_0(u) du.\] If \(z\sim q_\phi(z\mid x)\) and \(u=f_\psi^{-1}(z)\), the change-of-variables formula for the pushforward of \(q_\phi\) under \(f_\psi^{-1}\) is \[r_{\phi,\psi}(u\mid x) =q_\phi\left(f_\psi(u)\mid x\right) |\det J_{f_\psi}(u)|.\] Then \[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi\| p_\psi) &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\left[\log q_\phi-\log p_\psi\right] =\mathbb{E}_{r}\left[\log r-\log p_0\right] =\mathrm{KL}(r\| p_0), \end{aligned}\] because the Jacobian factors in \(\log q_\phi(f(u))\) and \(\log p_\psi(f(u))\) cancel against those in \(\log r\) and \(\log p_0\). Reconstruction expectations also match under \(z=f(u)\), so the ELBOs agree whenever the variational densities are exact images of each other.

For the counter-example, \(\det J_f=1\) (Chapter Matrix calculus and Jacobians), and \[r(u\mid x)\propto\exp\left(-\frac12 u_1^2-\frac12(u_2+u_1^2)^2\right).\] The coordinates are dependent, so \(r\) is not a product of univariate Gaussians. No diagonal-Gaussian encoder in \(u\)-space represents \(r\). Optimizing over diagonal Gaussians in \(z\) and in \(u\) are therefore different problems, even though the generative models are the same.

Homework question: Homework 2, problem 4.1

Let \(h=(h_A,h_B)\in\mathbb{R}^D\) with dimensions \(d_A+d_B=D\). Consider the affine coupling \[\begin{aligned} y_A&=h_A,\\ y_B&=h_B\odot\exp\left(s_\theta(h_A)\right)+t_\theta(h_A). \end{aligned}\] Let \(h\sim p_0\) and \(y=f_\theta(h)\). Prove that \(f_\theta\) is invertible and that \[\log p_\theta(y) =\log p_0\left(f_\theta^{-1}(y)\right) -\sum_{j=1}^{d_B}s_{\theta,j}(y_A).\] Give the inverse. Explain why the determinant stays tractable when \(s_\theta\) and \(t_\theta\) are neural networks.

Homework answer: Homework 2, problem 4.1

The inverse is \[\begin{aligned} h_A&=y_A,\\ h_B&=\left(y_B-t_\theta(y_A)\right)\odot\exp\left(-s_\theta(y_A)\right). \end{aligned}\] Ordering coordinates as \((A,B)\), the Jacobian is block triangular, \[J=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\ \ast&\operatorname{diag}\left(e^{s_\theta(h_A)}\right)\end{pmatrix},\] so \(\log|\det J|=\sum_{j=1}^{d_B}s_{\theta,j}(h_A)=\sum_{j=1}^{d_B}s_{\theta,j}(y_A)\). Change of variables then yields the claimed density formula. The networks \(s_\theta\) and \(t_\theta\) enter the map, but only the scale outputs appear in \(\det J\); their internal Jacobians never appear. That is why large networks are allowed.

Homework question: Homework 2, problem 4.2

A flow defines a density \(p_\theta(y)\) on \(\mathbb{R}^D\). For a discrete observation \(x\in\mathbb{Z}^D\) define the bin \(B_x=x+[0,1)^D\). Let \(y\sim p_\theta\) and \(x=\lfloor y\rfloor\). Prove that \[P_\theta(x)=\int_{[0,1)^D}p_\theta(x+u) du,\] that \(P_\theta(x)\ge 0\) and \(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^D}P_\theta(x)=1\), and explain the distinction between the density value \(p_\theta(x)\) and the mass \(P_\theta(x)\).

Homework answer: Homework 2, problem 4.2

The event \(\{\lfloor y\rfloor=x\}\) is exactly \(y\in B_x=x+[0,1)^D\), so \[P_\theta(x)=\int_{B_x}p_\theta(y) dy=\int_{[0,1)^D}p_\theta(x+u) du.\] Nonnegativity follows from \(p_\theta\ge 0\). The bins partition \(\mathbb{R}^D\) up to boundaries of measure zero, hence \[\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^D}P_\theta(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^D}p_\theta(y) dy=1.\] Thus \(P_\theta\) is a pmf on \(\mathbb{Z}^D\). The number \(p_\theta(x)\) is a density evaluated at a corner of the bin; it is not equal to the integral \(P_\theta(x)\).

Homework question: Homework 2, problem 5.1

Let \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times H\times W}\) and \(Y_{:,i,j}=W X_{:,i,j}\) with the same \(W\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times C}\) at every spatial site. <ol><li><p>Prove the map is invertible if and only if \(\det W\neq 0\), with inverse \(X_{:,i,j}=W^{-1}Y_{:,i,j}\).</p></li><li><p>Derive the full Jacobian \(J\) and prove \(\log|\det J|=HW\log|\det W|\).</p></li></ol>

Homework answer: Homework 2, problem 5.1

(a) At each site the map is left-multiplication by \(W\). It is bijective on \(\mathbb{R}^C\) if and only if \(W\) is invertible, i.e. \(\det W\neq 0\), and the inverse applies \(W^{-1}\) at every \((i,j)\). The global map is therefore invertible exactly under that condition.

(b) Vectorize so that the \(C\) channels of each pixel stay together. The map acts independently on each of the \(HW\) spatial sites, so \(J\) is block diagonal with \(HW\) copies of \(W\). Hence \[\det J=(\det W)^{HW},\qquad \log|\det J|=HW\log|\det W|.\]
