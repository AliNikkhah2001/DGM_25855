Hierarchical latents

One latent layer can be too weak a prior. A two-layer model draws a coarse code and then a fine code: \[p_\theta(x,z_1,z_2)=p_\theta(z_2) p_\theta(z_1\mid z_2) p_\theta(x\mid z_1),\] with the conditional independence \(x\perp z_2\mid z_1\). The variational family used in the homework respects the same order, \[q_\phi(z_1,z_2\mid x)=q_\phi(z_2\mid x) q_\phi(z_1\mid z_2,x).\]

The hierarchical ELBO

Apply to the pair \((z_1,z_2)\). The gap is \[\log p_\theta(x)-\mathcal L_H(x) =\mathrm{KL}\left(q(z_1,z_2\mid x)\| p(z_1,z_2\mid x)\right).\] Expand the joint and the variational factorizations. The cross terms separate into three pieces: \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal L_H(x) &=\mathbb{E}_{q(z_1,z_2\mid x)}[\log p(x\mid z_1)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(q(z_2\mid x)\| p(z_2)\right)\\ &\quad-\mathbb{E}_{q(z_2\mid x)}\left[\mathrm{KL}\left(q(z_1\mid z_2,x)\| p(z_1\mid z_2)\right)\right]. \end{aligned}\] The inner KL is averaged under \(q(z_2\mid x)\) because \(z_2\) is still random when that conditional KL is evaluated. You draw the coarse code from the approximate posterior, then score the fine code.

Comparison with a one-layer bound

Let \(q(z_1\mid x)\) be the marginal of the variational joint, and define the ordinary ELBO that uses only \(z_1\), \[\mathcal L_M(x) =\mathbb{E}_{q(z_1\mid x)}[\log p(x\mid z_1)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(q(z_1\mid x)\| p(z_1)\right).\] A second KL decomposition, this time of \(q(z_2\mid z_1,x)\) against \(p(z_2\mid z_1)\), yields \[\mathcal L_H(x) =\mathcal L_M(x) -\mathbb{E}_{q(z_1\mid x)}\left[\mathrm{KL}\left(q(z_2\mid z_1,x)\| p(z_2\mid z_1)\right)\right].\] The subtracted term is nonnegative, so \(\mathcal L_H\le\mathcal L_M\). Equality holds if and only if \(q(z_2\mid z_1,x)=p(z_2\mid z_1)\) almost everywhere. For a fixed marginal \(q(z_1\mid x)\), that conditional is exactly the one that maximizes \(\mathcal L_H\).

The marginal prior \(p(z_1)=\int p(z_1\mid z_2)p(z_2) dz_2\) may have no closed form. For an arbitrary positive auxiliary \(r(z_2\mid z_1)\), \[\begin{aligned} \log p(z_1) &=\mathbb{E}_{r}[\log p(z_1\mid z_2)]-\mathrm{KL}(r(z_2\mid z_1)\| p(z_2))\\ &\quad+\mathrm{KL}(r(z_2\mid z_1)\| p(z_2\mid z_1)). \end{aligned}\] Drop the last KL, which is nonnegative, and you have a lower bound. The bound is an equality when \(r=p(z_2\mid z_1)\). Therefore \[\log p(z_1) =\sup_r\left\{\mathbb{E}_r[\log p(z_1\mid z_2)]-\mathrm{KL}(r\| p(z_2))\right\},\] and the supremum is attained at the true conditional. This is the same ``gap equals a KL'' pattern as .

Practice

In the decomposition of \(\mathcal L_H\), why is the fine-code KL inside an expectation over \(q(z_2\mid x)\), rather than evaluated at a single \(z_2\)?

Worked solution

The KL \(\mathrm{KL}(q(z_1\mid z_2,x)\| p(z_1\mid z_2))\) is a function of \(z_2\). The variational joint draws \(z_2\) from \(q(z_2\mid x)\) before drawing \(z_1\). Taking the expectation is what the joint KL produces when you integrate that coordinate. Freezing one \(z_2\) would describe a different, degenerate inference distribution.

Homework question: Homework 2, problem 2.1

Consider a two-layer hierarchical VAE with \(x\perp z_2\mid z_1\), \[p_\theta(x,z_1,z_2)=p_\theta(z_2) p_\theta(z_1\mid z_2) p_\theta(x\mid z_1),\] and variational family \[q_\phi(z_1,z_2\mid x)=q_\phi(z_2\mid x) q_\phi(z_1\mid z_2,x).\] Starting from the KL between the approximate and true posteriors, prove that \[\log p_\theta(x) =\mathcal L_H(x) +\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z_1,z_2\mid x)\| p_\theta(z_1,z_2\mid x)\right).\] Derive the hierarchical ELBO in the decomposed form \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal L_H(x) &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z_1,z_2\mid x)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z_1)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z_2\mid x)\| p_\theta(z_2)\right)\\ &\quad-\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z_2\mid x)}\left[ \mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z_1\mid z_2,x)\| p_\theta(z_1\mid z_2)\right) \right]. \end{aligned}\] Express \(\log p_\theta(x)-\mathcal L_H(x)\) as a KL, and explain why the final KL is averaged under \(q_\phi(z_2\mid x)\).

Homework answer: Homework 2, problem 2.1

Apply the ELBO–KL identity to the joint latent \((z_1,z_2)\): \[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z_1,z_2\mid x)\| p_\theta(z_1,z_2\mid x)\right) &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\left[ \log q_\phi-\log p_\theta(x,z_1,z_2)+\log p_\theta(x) \right]\\ &=\log p_\theta(x)-\mathcal L_H(x), \end{aligned}\] where \[\mathcal L_H(x) =\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z_1,z_2\mid x)}\left[ \log\frac{p_\theta(x,z_1,z_2)}{q_\phi(z_1,z_2\mid x)} \right].\] Hence \(\log p_\theta(x)-\mathcal L_H(x)\) equals that joint KL.

Substitute the factorizations \(p_\theta(x,z_1,z_2)=p_\theta(z_2) p_\theta(z_1\mid z_2) p_\theta(x\mid z_1)\) and \(q_\phi=q_\phi(z_2\mid x) q_\phi(z_1\mid z_2,x)\): \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal L_H &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\left[ \log p(x\mid z_1)+\log p(z_2)+\log p(z_1\mid z_2)\\ &\qquad-\log q(z_2\mid x)-\log q(z_1\mid z_2,x) \right]. \end{aligned}\] The terms in \(z_2\) alone rearrange to \(-\mathrm{KL}(q(z_2\mid x)\| p(z_2))\). The terms involving \(z_1\) given \(z_2\) rearrange to \[-\mathbb{E}_{q(z_2\mid x)}\left[ \mathrm{KL}\left(q(z_1\mid z_2,x)\| p(z_1\mid z_2)\right) \right],\] because the conditional KL is a function of the random \(z_2\sim q(z_2\mid x)\). The remaining piece is \(\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log p(x\mid z_1)]\). That is the claimed decomposition. The outer expectation on the fine-code KL is forced by the joint variational law: \(z_2\) is drawn from \(q(z_2\mid x)\) before \(z_1\) is scored.

Prove that \[\mathcal L_H(x) =\mathcal L_M(x) -\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z_1\mid x)}\left[ \mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z_2\mid z_1,x)\| p_\theta(z_2\mid z_1)\right) \right],\] where \[\mathcal L_M(x) =\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z_1\mid x)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z_1)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z_1\mid x)\| p_\theta(z_1)\right).\] Prove \(\mathcal L_H\le\mathcal L_M\), give the necessary and sufficient condition for equality, and characterize the conditional \(q(z_2\mid z_1,x)\) that maximizes \(\mathcal L_H\) for fixed marginal \(q_\phi(z_1\mid x)\).

For an arbitrary auxiliary \(r(z_2\mid z_1)\), prove \[\begin{aligned} \log p_\theta(z_1) &=\mathbb{E}_r[\log p_\theta(z_1\mid z_2)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(r(z_2\mid z_1)\| p_\theta(z_2)\right)\\ &\quad+\mathrm{KL}\left(r(z_2\mid z_1)\| p_\theta(z_2\mid z_1)\right). \end{aligned}\] Hence obtain the variational representation \[\log p_\theta(z_1) =\sup_r\left\{ \mathbb{E}_r[\log p_\theta(z_1\mid z_2)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(r\| p_\theta(z_2)\right) \right\},\] and determine when the supremum is attained.

Homework answer: Homework 2, problem 2.2

Write the hierarchical ELBO with the reverse factorization of \(q\), namely \(q(z_1,z_2\mid x)=q(z_1\mid x) q(z_2\mid z_1,x)\), and expand \(p(z_1,z_2)=p(z_1) p(z_2\mid z_1)\): \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal L_H &=\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x\mid z_1)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(q(z_1\mid x)\| p(z_1)\right)\\ &\quad-\mathbb{E}_{q(z_1\mid x)}\left[ \mathrm{KL}\left(q(z_2\mid z_1,x)\| p(z_2\mid z_1)\right) \right]\\ &=\mathcal L_M(x) -\mathbb{E}_{q(z_1\mid x)}\left[ \mathrm{KL}\left(q(z_2\mid z_1,x)\| p(z_2\mid z_1)\right) \right]. \end{aligned}\] The subtracted KL is nonnegative, so \(\mathcal L_H\le\mathcal L_M\). Equality holds if and only if \(q(z_2\mid z_1,x)=p(z_2\mid z_1)\) almost everywhere. For fixed marginal \(q(z_1\mid x)\), that conditional is exactly the maximizer of \(\mathcal L_H\).

For the prior identity, expand \[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{KL}\left(r(z_2\mid z_1)\| p(z_2\mid z_1)\right) &=\mathbb{E}_r[\log r-\log p(z_1,z_2)+\log p(z_1)]\\ &=\mathbb{E}_r[\log r-\log p(z_2)-\log p(z_1\mid z_2)]+\log p(z_1). \end{aligned}\] Rearrangement gives \[\begin{aligned} \log p(z_1) &=\mathbb{E}_r[\log p(z_1\mid z_2)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(r\| p(z_2)\right) +\mathrm{KL}\left(r\| p(z_2\mid z_1)\right). \end{aligned}\] Drop the last nonnegative KL to obtain a lower bound. The bound is an equality when \(r=p(z_2\mid z_1)\), so \[\log p(z_1) =\sup_r\left\{ \mathbb{E}_r[\log p(z_1\mid z_2)]-\mathrm{KL}(r\| p(z_2)) \right\},\] with the supremum attained at the true conditional \(r=p(z_2\mid z_1)\).
