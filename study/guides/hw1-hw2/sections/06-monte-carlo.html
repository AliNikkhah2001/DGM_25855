Monte Carlo

The sample average

Definition

If \(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(N)}\) are drawn independently from \(p\), the Monte Carlo estimator of \(\mathbb{E}_p[f]\) is \[\hat f_N=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N f(x^{(n)}).\] It is unbiased. If \(\mathrm{Var}_p(f)=\sigma_f^2<\infty\), then \(\mathrm{Var}(\hat f_N)=\sigma_f^2/N\).

Intuition

Unbiased means the average of the estimator, over datasets of samples, equals the integral. It does not mean one sample is exact. The error typically shrinks like \(1/\sqrt{N}\), because the standard deviation is \(\sigma_f/\sqrt{N}\). Lecture 2, slides 34–35, and Homework 1, problem 2, turn that variance into a sample size through Chebyshev or a Chernoff bound. Chebyshev uses only the variance. A Chernoff bound uses the moment generating function and can demand fewer samples while remaining a guarantee.

Importance sampling

When draws from \(p\) are unavailable, draw \(x\sim q\) instead and correct the weight: \[\mathbb{E}_p[f]=\mathbb{E}_q\left[f(x)\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right],\] provided \(q\) is positive wherever \(p f\) is nonzero. The weight \(p/q\) is the importance weight. The estimator is unbiased for any such \(q\). Its variance is small when \(q\) is large where \(| f| p\) is large, and it is huge when \(q\) is tiny on that set.

Watch out

Lecture 4, slides 23–29, shows a proposal that misses the posterior mass. The likelihood estimator then has rare enormous weights. The ELBO replaces that high-variance estimator by a bound whose gap is exactly a KL. You give up unbiasedness of the likelihood and gain a gradient you can train.

Perplexity

For a discrete model, perplexity is \(\exp\left(-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}\log p_\theta(x)\right)\), or the same expression with the average over a finite test set. It is an exponential of a cross-entropy. Jensen applied to the exponential, or a bias expansion of the logarithm of an average, shows that a naive plug-in can be biased. Homework 1 asks for that expansion. The point to remember is that the log of an average is not the average of the logs.
