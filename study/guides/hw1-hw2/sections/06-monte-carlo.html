Monte Carlo

The sample average

Definition

If \(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(N)}\) are drawn independently from \(p\), the Monte Carlo estimator of \(\mathbb{E}_p[f]\) is \[\hat f_N=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N f(x^{(n)}).\] It is unbiased. If \(\mathrm{Var}_p(f)=\sigma_f^2<\infty\), then \(\mathrm{Var}(\hat f_N)=\sigma_f^2/N\).

Intuition

Unbiased means the average of the estimator, over datasets of samples, equals the integral. It does not mean one sample is exact. The error typically shrinks like \(1/\sqrt{N}\), because the standard deviation is \(\sigma_f/\sqrt{N}\). Lecture 2, slides 34–35, and Homework 1, problem 2, turn that variance into a sample size through Chebyshev or a Chernoff bound. Chebyshev uses only the variance. A Chernoff bound uses the moment generating function and can demand fewer samples while remaining a guarantee.

Result: Chebyshev sample size

If \(\sigma_f^2<\infty\), then for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \[\mathbb P\left(|\hat f_N-\mathbb{E}_p f|\ge\varepsilon\right)\le\frac{\sigma_f^2}{N\varepsilon^2}.\] The right-hand side tends to \(0\) (weak law). The sample size needed for deviation \(\varepsilon\) and failure probability \(\delta\) is \(N\ge\sigma_f^2/(\delta\varepsilon^2)\). Root-mean-square error is \(O(N^{-1/2})\).

A Chernoff bound replaces the variance by the moment generating function. For a \(\chi^2_N\) law the mgf is \((1-2\lambda)^{-N/2}\) for \(\lambda<1/2\), and optimizing Markov's inequality on each tail produces explicit rate functions \(I_\pm(t)\). The resulting \(N\) is still a finite-sample guarantee, typically tighter than Chebyshev and looser than a CLT quantile (which is only asymptotic).

Importance sampling

When draws from \(p\) are unavailable, draw \(x\sim q\) instead and correct the weight: \[\mathbb{E}_p[f]=\mathbb{E}_q\left[f(x)\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right],\] provided \(q\) is positive wherever \(p f\) is nonzero. The weight \(p/q\) is the importance weight. The estimator is unbiased for any such \(q\). Its variance is small when \(q\) is large where \(| f| p\) is large, and it is huge when \(q\) is tiny on that set.

Result: Second-moment criterion

The one-sample variance \(\mathrm{Var}_q(fp/q)\) is finite if and only if \(\mathbb{E}_q[(fp/q)^2]<\infty\). Among nonnegative integrands, the zero-variance proposal is \(q^\star(x)\propto f(x)p(x)\), which requires the unknown integral as a normalizer and is therefore not a practical proposal.

For the scalar example \(f(x)=x^2\), \(p=\mathcal{N}(0,1)\), and Gaussian proposals \(q=\mathcal{N}(0,s^2)\), the estimator is unbiased for every \(s>0\), the second moment is finite if and only if \(s>1/\sqrt 2\), and the variance-minimizing scale among Gaussians is \(s=\sqrt 3\).

Watch out

Lecture 4, slides 23–29, shows a proposal that misses the posterior mass. The likelihood estimator then has rare enormous weights. The ELBO replaces that high-variance estimator by a bound whose gap is exactly a KL. You give up unbiasedness of the likelihood and gain a gradient you can train.

Perplexity

For a discrete model, perplexity is \(\exp\left(-\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}\log p_\theta(x)\right)\), or the same expression with the average over a finite test set. It is an exponential of a cross-entropy. Write \(\ell(x)=-\log p_\theta(x)\), \(\mathrm{PPL}=\exp(\mathbb{E}[\ell])\), and \(\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N=\exp(\bar\ell_N)\) with \(\bar\ell_N=N^{-1}\sum_n\ell(x_n)\).

Result: Bias and consistency of plug-in perplexity

Jensen for the convex map \(\exp\) gives \(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N]\ge\mathrm{PPL}\) for every finite \(N\), with equality if and only if \(\ell\) is \(p_{\mathrm{data}}\)-a.s. constant. If \(\mathbb{E}|\ell|<\infty\), the weak law and continuity of \(\exp\) give consistency in probability. Under a third-moment assumption the bias admits the expansion \[\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N]=\mathrm{PPL}\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\ell)}{2N}+o(N^{-1})\right).\]

When data are Gaussian and the model is \(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\) with variance ratio \(r=\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{data}}/\sigma^2\), the plug-in has a closed form involving the chi-squared mgf, finite if and only if \(N>r\), and the asymptotic coefficient matches \(\mathrm{Var}(\ell)/2=r^2/4\).

Homework 1 asks for that expansion. The point to remember is that the log of an average is not the average of the logs—here the exponential of an average is biased upward.

Homework question: Homework 1, problem 2.1

Show that the Monte Carlo average \(f_N=N^{-1}\sum_{n\le N}f(x_n)\) is unbiased when \(\mathbb{E}_p|f|<\infty\), compute its variance when \(\mathrm{Var}_p(f)=\sigma_f^2<\infty\), and deduce Chebyshev's bound and the weak law.

Homework answer: Homework 1, problem 2.1

If \(\mathbb{E}_p|f|<\infty\), then \(\mathbb{E}[f_N]=\mathbb{E}_p[f]\), so \(f_N\) is unbiased. If \(\mathrm{Var}_p(f)=\sigma_f^2<\infty\), independence gives \(\mathrm{Var}(f_N)=\sigma_f^2/N\).

Chebyshev: for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \[\mathbb P\big(|f_N-\mathbb{E}_p f|\ge\varepsilon\big)\le\frac{\sigma_f^2}{N\varepsilon^2}.\] The right-hand side tends to \(0\), which is the weak law, with rate \(O(1/N)\) in probability deviation and \(O(N^{-1/2})\) in root-mean-square error. The sample size needed for deviation \(\varepsilon\) and probability \(\delta\) is \(N\ge\sigma_f^2/(\delta\varepsilon^2)\).

Homework question: Homework 1, problem 2.1(a)

Let \(X\sim\mathcal{N}(1,4)\) and \(f(X)=(X-1)^2\). Bound or approximate the \(N\) needed so that \(\mathbb P(|f_N-4|\ge 1/2)\le 0.05\) by (i) Chebyshev, (ii) CLT, and (iii) Chernoff on the underlying chi-squared. Round Chernoff to the nearest \(50\), and compare the three answers.

Homework answer: Homework 1, problem 2.1(a)

Let \(Z=(X-1)/2\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\). Then \(f(X)=4Z^2\), so \(\mathbb{E}[f]=4\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(f)=16\mathrm{Var}(Z^2)=16(3-1)=32\).

Each \((X_n-1)^2/4\sim\chi^2_1\), hence \(f_N=(4/N)\chi^2_N\). The event \(|f_N-4|\ge 1/2\) is \(|\chi^2_N-N|\ge N/8\), i.e. a relative deviation \(t=1/8\).

(i) Chebyshev. \(\mathrm{Var}(f_N)=32/N\), so the probability is at most \(32/(N\cdot 1/4)=128/N\). The bound is at most \(0.05\) once \(N\ge 128/0.05=2560\). This \(N\) is guaranteed.

(ii) CLT. Use \(\sqrt{32/N}\) as the standard deviation and the normal quantile \(1.96\): \[\frac{1/2}{\sqrt{32/N}}\ge 1.96 \iff N\ge 32\cdot(3.92)^2=491.72\ldots\] So \(N\ge 492\). This is an approximation, not a finite-sample guarantee.

(iii) Chernoff. The mgf of \(\chi^2_N\) is \((1-2\lambda)^{-N/2}\) for \(\lambda<1/2\). Optimizing the Markov bound on each tail produces, for \(t\in(0,1)\), \[\begin{aligned} \mathbb P(\chi^2_N\ge N(1+t))&\le\big((1+t)e^{-t}\big)^{N/2}=e^{-NI_+(t)},\\ \mathbb P(\chi^2_N\le N(1-t))&\le\big((1-t)e^{t}\big)^{N/2}=e^{-NI_-(t)}, \end{aligned}\] with \(I_+(t)=\frac12(t-\log(1+t))\) and \(I_-(t)=\frac12(-\log(1-t)-t)\). At \(t=1/8\), \(I_+\approx 0.0036085\) and \(I_-\approx 0.0042657\). The sum of the two tails drops to \(0.05\) or below at \(N=950\). Nearest \(50\) is \(950\). This \(N\) is guaranteed.

Chebyshev and Chernoff are theorems about this finite \(N\); the CLT is only an asymptotic shape. Chebyshev is loose (\(2560\)). Chernoff is tighter (\(950\)) but still a bound. The CLT suggests about \(492\) and is the most optimistic precisely because it is not guaranteed.

Homework question: Homework 1, problem 2.1(b)

Importance-sample \(\mathbb{E}_p[x^2]=1\) for \(p=\mathcal{N}(0,1)\) with proposals \(q=\mathcal{N}(0,s^2)\). Show unbiasedness for every \(s>0\), find when the variance is finite, minimize that variance over \(s\), and identify the zero-variance proposal over all densities.

Homework answer: Homework 1, problem 2.1(b)

\[\mathbb{E}_q\Big[f(x)\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\Big]=\int x^2 p(x) dx=1\] whenever \(s>0\), because \(q>0\) everywhere and \(x^2p\) is integrable. So \(\hat I_N\) is unbiased for every \(s>0\), and \(\mathrm{Var}(\hat I_N)=N^{-1}\mathrm{Var}_q(fp/q)\).

The second moment is finite if and only if \(s>1/\sqrt 2\). Indeed \[\frac{p(x)^2}{q(x)}=s(2\pi)^{-1/2}\exp\big(-x^2(1-\frac1{2s^2})\big),\] and \(\int x^4 p^2/q\) converges precisely when the quadratic rate \(1-1/(2s^2)\) is positive. Evaluating the Gaussian moment gives \[\mathbb{E}_q\Big[\Big(x^2\frac pq\Big)^2\Big]=\frac{3s^6}{(2s^2-1)^{5/2}},\qquad \mathrm{Var}_q\Big(x^2\frac pq\Big)=\frac{3s^6}{(2s^2-1)^{5/2}}-1.\] Minimize \(v(u)=3u^3/(2u-1)^{5/2}\) for \(u=s^2>1/2\). The logarithmic derivative \(3/u-5/(2u-1)\) vanishes at \(u=3\), and \(v\to\infty\) at both endpoints, so the best Gaussian scale is \(s=\sqrt 3\), with minimal one-sample variance \((81\sqrt 5-125)/125\approx 0.44897\). Ordinary Monte Carlo (\(s=1\)) has variance \(2\).

Over all proposals, \(f\ge 0\), so the zero-variance proposal is \(q^\star(x)\propto f(x)p(x)=x^2 p(x)\). It is already normalized because \(\mathbb{E}_p[x^2]=1\): \[q^\star(x)=x^2\cdot(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/2}.\] Then \(fp/q^\star=1\) for \(x\ne 0\), so the importance-sampling estimator has variance \(0\). Using it requires the unknown integral as a normalizing constant.

Homework question: Homework 1, problem 2.2

Define \(\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N=\exp(\bar\ell_N)\) with \(\bar\ell_N=N^{-1}\sum_{n\le N}\ell(x_n)\) and \(\mathrm{PPL}=\exp(\mathbb{E}[\ell])\). Show that the estimator is biased upward for every finite \(N\), characterize equality, and prove consistency when \(\mathbb{E}|\ell|<\infty\).

Homework answer: Homework 1, problem 2.2

Jensen's inequality for the convex map \(\exp\) gives \[\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N]\ge\exp(\mathbb{E}[\bar\ell_N])=\exp(\mathbb{E}[\ell])=\mathrm{PPL},\] for every finite \(N\). Equality holds if and only if \(\bar\ell_N\) is almost surely constant, i.e. \(\ell(x)\) is \(p_{\mathrm{data}}\)-a.s. constant. In particular \(\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N\) is not unbiased unless \(\ell\) is a.s. constant.

If \(\mathbb{E}|\ell|<\infty\), the weak law gives \(\bar\ell_N\to\mathbb{E}[\ell]\) in probability, and continuity of \(\exp\) gives \(\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N\to\mathrm{PPL}\) in probability. The estimator is consistent. The strong law upgrades this to almost-sure convergence.

Homework question: Homework 1, problem 2.2(a)

Assuming \(\mathbb{E}|\ell|^3<\infty\), expand \(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N]/\mathrm{PPL}\) through order \(1/N\) and identify the coefficient \(c=\mathrm{Var}(\ell)/2\).

Homework answer: Homework 1, problem 2.2(a)

Write \(\delta_N=\bar\ell_N-\mathbb{E}[\ell]\), so \(\mathbb{E}\delta_N=0\), \(\mathbb{E}\delta_N^2=\mathrm{Var}(\ell)/N\), and \(\mathbb{E}\delta_N^3=O(1/N^2)\). Then \[\mathbb{E}[e^{\delta_N}]=1+\frac12\mathbb{E}[\delta_N^2]+O(1/N^2)=1+\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\ell)}{2N}+o(N^{-1}).\] Hence \(c=\mathrm{Var}(\ell)/2\).

Homework question: Homework 1, problem 2.2(b)

For Gaussian data of variance \(s^2\) and model \(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\) with \(r=s^2/\sigma^2\), derive the closed form of \(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N]\), the condition \(N>r\) for finiteness, the asymptotic coefficient \(c=r^2/4\), and the interpretation when \(r>1\).

Homework answer: Homework 1, problem 2.2(b)

\[\ell(x)=\frac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)+\frac{x^2}{2\sigma^2},\qquad \mathbb{E}[\ell]=\frac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)+\frac r2,\] so \(\mathrm{PPL}=\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2} e^{r/2}\). With \(\sum_n x_n^2=s^2\chi^2_N\), \[\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N=\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2} \exp\big(\frac{r}{2N}\chi^2_N\big).\] The chi-squared mgf at \(\lambda=r/(2N)\) exists if and only if \(r/N<1\), i.e. \(N>r\), and then \[\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N]=\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2} (1-r/N)^{-N/2} =\mathrm{PPL}\cdot e^{-r/2}(1-r/N)^{-N/2}.\] For \(N\le r\) the expectation is infinite.

Expand \(\log(1-r/N)=-r/N-r^2/(2N^2)+O(N^{-3})\): \[(1-r/N)^{-N/2}=e^{r/2}\big(1+\frac{r^2}{4N}+O(N^{-2})\big).\] So \(c=r^2/4\). Independently, \(\mathrm{Var}(x^2)=2s^4\) and \(\mathrm{Var}(\ell)=r^2/2\), hence \(\mathrm{Var}(\ell)/2=r^2/4\), matching problem 2.2(a).

If \(r>1\), the model variance is smaller than the data variance. Then \(\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{PPL}}_N]=\infty\) for every \(N\le r\): rare large data points make \(-\log p_\theta(x)\) so heavy-tailed that the exponential moment does not exist. Consistency is only visible once \(N>r\).
