How to use this guide

This note teaches the mathematics that Homeworks 1 and 2 actually use. Each chapter states a definition, gives the intuition, derives the identity, draws the picture, and then poses a short practice problem with a worked solution. The practice problems are smaller than the homework. They train the same moves. Full Homework 1 theory questions and solutions appear as hwqbox/hwsolbox pairs at the ends of Chapters Divergences–Autoregressive models.

Intuition: What to read for which homework

Homework 1 uses Chapters Probability–Autoregressive models. Homework 2 uses those chapters and then Chapters Variational autoencoders–Normalizing flows. The conditional ELBO, the hierarchical ELBO, dequantization, and the Glow convolution are extensions of the lectures. Their derivations are written out here.

Definition: Notation used throughout

Random variables are capitals when the distinction matters, and the same letter is used for a value when the slides do so. A Gaussian \(\mathcal{N}(x\mid\mu,\sigma^2)\) has variance \(\sigma^2\), matching Lecture 2. \(\log\) is the natural logarithm, so information quantities are in nats. \(\mathrm{KL}(q\| p)\) means we use \(q\) where the reference in the denominator is \(p\). The Jacobian \(J_f(u)\) is the matrix of partial derivatives \(\partial f_i/\partial u_j\).
