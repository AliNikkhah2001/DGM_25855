Practice: Set, problem A

Let \(q=\mathcal{N}(0,\operatorname{diag}(1,e))\) and \(p=\mathcal{N}(0,I_2)\). Compute \(\mathrm{KL}(q\| p)\) from .

Worked solution

Coordinate \(1\) contributes \(0\). Coordinate \(2\) has \(\mu=0\) and \(\sigma^2=e\), so \(\frac12(e-1-\log e)=\frac12(e-2)\). The sum is \((e-2)/2\).

Practice: Set, problem B

Show that if \(p(x\mid z)=p(x)\) does not depend on \(z\), and \(q(z)=p(z)\), then the ELBO equals \(\log p(x)\).

Worked solution

The reconstruction term is \(\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x)]=\log p(x)\). The KL against the prior is zero. This is the collapsed case: the bound is tight because the latent was never used.

Practice: Set, problem C

Give the inverse and the log-absolute-determinant of \(y_1=h_1\), \(y_2=h_2 e^{h_1}+h_1^2\).

Worked solution

\(h_1=y_1\) and \(h_2=(y_2-y_1^2)e^{-y_1}\). The Jacobian is triangular with diagonal \((1, e^{h_1})\), so the log-absolute-determinant is \(h_1=y_1\).

Practice: Set, problem D

On the bin \([0,1)\), let \(p(x+u)=c e^{u}\) for \(u\in[0,1)\), with \(c\) chosen so that you do not need its value. Is uniform dequantization tight?

Worked solution

No. Tightness needs \(p(x+u)\) constant in \(u\). Here it grows with \(u\). The optimal \(q\) is proportional to \(e^{u}\) on the bin.

Practice: Set, problem E

A \(1\times 1\) convolution uses \(W=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\) on a \(3\times 4\) feature map with \(2\) channels. Compute \(\log|\det J|\).

Worked solution

\(W\) is a permutation, so \(|\det W|=1\) and the log-determinant is zero. The spatial factor \(HW=12\) multiplies zero. Permutations mix channels and do not change volume. Glow's learned diagonal \(s\) is what changes volume.
