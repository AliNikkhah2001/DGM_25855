Variational autoencoders

The model

A latent-variable model says that each observation \(x\) was produced by a hidden \(z\): \[p_\theta(x,z)=p_\theta(x\mid z) p_\theta(z),\qquad p_\theta(x)=\int p_\theta(x\mid z) p_\theta(z) dz.\] Generation draws \(z\) from the prior and then \(x\) from the decoder. The integral is the obstacle. It is the marginal likelihood.

For a conditional model the sheet adds a variable \(u\), for example a digit label: \[p_\theta(x,z\mid u)=p_\theta(x\mid z,u) p_\theta(z\mid u).\] Every derivation below is the unconditional one with \(u\) written next to \(x\). The pictures gain arrows from \(u\) into the prior and into the decoder.

Two derivations of the ELBO

Fix a density \(q(z)\) that does not depend on \(\theta\), positive wherever the posterior is. The marginal is an expectation under \(q\): \[p_\theta(x)=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim q}\left[\frac{p_\theta(x,z)}{q(z)}\right].\] Apply \(\log\) and Jensen. The logarithm is concave, so \[ \log p_\theta(x) \ge \mathbb{E}_{z\sim q}\left[\log\frac{p_\theta(x,z)}{q(z)}\right] =:\mathcal L(x;\theta,q).\] The right-hand side is the evidence lower bound.

The second derivation starts from the KL between \(q\) and the true posterior \(p_\theta(z\mid x)=p_\theta(x,z)/p_\theta(x)\): \[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{KL}(q(z)\| p_\theta(z\mid x)) &=\mathbb{E}_q\left[\log q(z)-\log p_\theta(x,z)+\log p_\theta(x)\right]\\ &=\log p_\theta(x)-\mathbb{E}_q\left[\log\frac{p_\theta(x,z)}{q(z)}\right]. \end{aligned}\] Rearrangement is the identity on Lecture 4, slides 38–39, \[ \log p_\theta(x)=\mathcal L(x;\theta,q)+\mathrm{KL}\left(q(z)\| p_\theta(z\mid x)\right).\] Nonnegativity of KL gives \(\mathcal L\le\log p_\theta(x)\). The gap is zero if and only if \(q(z)=p_\theta(z\mid x)\) almost everywhere.

Intuition

Jensen moves the log inside and produces a bound. The KL identity explains the gap. When you write Homework 2, problem 1.1, do both, and insert \(u\) into \(p(x,z\mid u)\) and \(q(z\mid x,u)\). Equality is the sentence ``KL is zero only when the densities agree.''

Reconstruction minus KL

Expand \(p(x,z)=p(x\mid z)p(z)\) inside the ELBO: \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal L &=\mathbb{E}_q[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)+\log p_\theta(z)-\log q(z)]\\ &=\mathbb{E}_q[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)]-\mathrm{KL}\left(q(z)\| p_\theta(z)\right). \end{aligned}\] The first term rewards a decoder that regenerates \(x\) from the latent the encoder proposed. The second term penalizes an encoder that wanders away from the prior. For the conditional model, \[ \mathcal L(\theta,\phi;x,u) =\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x,u)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z,u)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z\mid x,u)\| p_\theta(z\mid u)\right).\]

Watch out: Posterior collapse

Suppose \(q(z\mid x,u)=p(z\mid u)\). The KL term in is zero. The gap that remains is \[\log p(x\mid u)-\mathcal L =\log\mathbb{E}_{p(z\mid u)}[p(x\mid z,u)] -\mathbb{E}_{p(z\mid u)}[\log p(x\mid z,u)].\] That is Jensen's gap for the random variable \(p(x\mid z,u)\) as \(z\) varies. It is strictly positive unless \(p(x\mid z,u)\) does not depend on \(z\).

If the decoder ignores \(z\), so that \(p(x\mid z,u)=p(x\mid u)\), Bayes' rule gives \(p(z\mid x,u)=p(z\mid u)\). Matching the encoder to the prior then makes both the KL and the gap vanish. The latent carries no information about \(x\). That is posterior collapse. The bound is tight and useless.

The reparameterization trick

The KL in has a gradient in closed form. The reconstruction term does not. A score-function estimator differentiates through \(\log q\) and has high variance. The reparameterization writes the sample as a deterministic function of the parameters and of noise that does not depend on them: \[\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),\qquad z=\mu_\phi(x)+\sigma_\phi(x)\odot\varepsilon,\] where \(\sigma=\exp(\frac12\log\sigma^2)\) if the network emits a log-variance. Because \(\varepsilon\) is standard normal, \(z\) is exactly \(\mathcal{N}(\mu,\operatorname{diag}(\sigma^2))\).

A Monte Carlo reconstruction objective with one sample is \[\widehat{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{rec}}(\phi) =\log p_\theta\left(x\mid \mu_\phi+\sigma_\phi\odot\varepsilon\right).\] The chain rule is \[\nabla_\phi \widehat{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{rec}} =\frac{\partial\log p_\theta}{\partial z} \left( \frac{\partial\mu}{\partial\phi} +\varepsilon\odot\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial\phi} \right).\] Differentiate through the sample, not through a density score. Lecture 4, slides 60–64, is this argument.

For images in the notebook the decoder outputs Bernoulli logits, one per pixel. The reconstruction term is a binary cross-entropy with logits, summed over pixels and averaged over the batch. Do not apply a sigmoid before that loss. The sigmoid sits inside the stable implementation of the cross-entropy. With \(\beta=1\) the training objective per image is that cross-entropy plus the KL in .

Closed-form KL in the conditional model

In a CVAE the regularization term is \(\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z\mid x,u)\| p_\theta(z\mid u)\right)\). When both densities are diagonal Gaussians on \(\mathbb{R}^d\), \[q_\phi(z\mid x,u)=\mathcal{N}(\mu_q,\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_q^2)),\qquad p_\theta(z\mid u)=\mathcal{N}(\mu_p,\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_p^2)),\] the KL factors across coordinates. Chapter Divergences derived the one-dimensional identity by expanding \(\mathbb{E}_q[\log q-\log p]\) and using \(\mathbb{E}_q[(z-\mu_q)^2]=\sigma_q^2\) together with \(\mathbb{E}_q[(z-\mu_p)^2]=\sigma_q^2+(\mu_q-\mu_p)^2\). Summing over dimensions recovers . Against the standard prior \(p_\theta(z\mid u)=\mathcal{N}(0,I)\) the same algebra collapses to . Homework question: Homework 2, problem 1.1

Consider a CVAE with observed \(x\), conditioning variable \(u\), and latent \(z\), \[p_\theta(x,z\mid u)=p_\theta(x\mid z,u) p_\theta(z\mid u).\] The goal is to maximize \(\log p_\theta(x\mid u)\). Approximate the intractable posterior by \(q_\phi(z\mid x,u)\).

Using Jensen's inequality, prove that \[\log p_\theta(x\mid u) \ge \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x,u)}\left[\log\frac{p_\theta(x,z\mid u)}{q_\phi(z\mid x,u)}\right] =:\mathcal L(\theta,\phi;x,u).\] Give a second, independent proof starting from \(\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z\mid x,u)\| p_\theta(z\mid x,u)\right)\), and show that \[\log p_\theta(x\mid u) =\mathcal L(\theta,\phi;x,u) +\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z\mid x,u)\| p_\theta(z\mid x,u)\right).\] Conclude that \(\mathcal L\le\log p_\theta(x\mid u)\), with equality if and only if \(q_\phi(z\mid x,u)=p_\theta(z\mid x,u)\) almost everywhere.

Homework answer: Homework 2, problem 1.1

Write the marginal as an expectation under \(q_\phi\): \[p_\theta(x\mid u) =\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x,u)}\left[\frac{p_\theta(x,z\mid u)}{q_\phi(z\mid x,u)}\right].\] Apply \(\log\) and Jensen (concave logarithm), exactly as in with \(u\) carried through: \[\log p_\theta(x\mid u) \ge \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\left[\log\frac{p_\theta(x,z\mid u)}{q_\phi(z\mid x,u)}\right] =\mathcal L(\theta,\phi;x,u).\]

For the second proof expand the KL against the true posterior \(p_\theta(z\mid x,u)=p_\theta(x,z\mid u)/p_\theta(x\mid u)\): \[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi\| p_\theta(z\mid x,u)\right) &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\left[ \log q_\phi(z\mid x,u) -\log p_\theta(x,z\mid u) +\log p_\theta(x\mid u) \right]\\ &=\log p_\theta(x\mid u)-\mathcal L(\theta,\phi;x,u). \end{aligned}\] Rearrangement is the conditional form of . Nonnegativity of KL yields \(\mathcal L\le\log p_\theta(x\mid u)\). Equality holds if and only if the two densities agree almost everywhere, which is the second property of KL in Chapter Divergences.

Homework question: Homework 2, problem 1.2

Prove the reconstruction–regularization decomposition \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal L(\theta,\phi;x,u) &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x,u)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z,u)]\\ &\quad-\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z\mid x,u)\| p_\theta(z\mid u)\right), \end{aligned}\] and briefly explain each term.

Suppose \(q_\phi(z\mid x,u)=p_\theta(z\mid u)\). Prove that the regularization term vanishes and that the ELBO gap becomes \[\log p_\theta(x\mid u)-\mathcal L =\log\mathbb{E}_{p_\theta(z\mid u)}[p_\theta(x\mid z,u)] -\mathbb{E}_{p_\theta(z\mid u)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z,u)].\] Show the gap is strictly positive whenever \(p_\theta(x\mid z,u)\) is not constant in \(z\).

Now suppose the decoder ignores the latent, \(p_\theta(x\mid z,u)=p_\theta(x\mid u)\). Prove that \(p_\theta(z\mid x,u)=p_\theta(z\mid u)\). Show that if the variational posterior also matches the conditional prior, both the regularization term and the ELBO gap vanish, and explain posterior collapse.

Homework answer: Homework 2, problem 1.2

Expand \(p_\theta(x,z\mid u)=p_\theta(x\mid z,u) p_\theta(z\mid u)\) inside the ELBO: \[\begin{aligned} \mathcal L &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z,u)+\log p_\theta(z\mid u)-\log q_\phi(z\mid x,u)]\\ &=\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z,u)] -\mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(z\mid x,u)\| p_\theta(z\mid u)\right), \end{aligned}\] which is . The first term is a reconstruction score: it rewards a decoder that regenerates \(x\) from the encoder's \(z\) (and from \(u\)). The second term is a regularizer: it keeps \(q_\phi( \cdot\mid x,u)\) near the conditional prior.

If \(q_\phi=p_\theta(z\mid u)\), the KL is zero. The gap identity then reads \[\begin{aligned} \log p_\theta(x\mid u)-\mathcal L &=\log\mathbb{E}_{p(z\mid u)}[p(x\mid z,u)] -\mathbb{E}_{p(z\mid u)}[\log p(x\mid z,u)], \end{aligned}\] dropping \(\theta\) for brevity. The right-hand side is Jensen's gap for the random variable \(p(x\mid z,u)\) as \(z\sim p(z\mid u)\). Strict concavity of \(\log\) makes the gap strictly positive unless \(p(x\mid z,u)\) is almost surely constant in \(z\).

If \(p(x\mid z,u)=p(x\mid u)\), Bayes' rule gives \[p(z\mid x,u) =\frac{p(x\mid z,u) p(z\mid u)}{p(x\mid u)} =p(z\mid u).\] Matching \(q_\phi\) to that prior zeros the regularization KL. The true posterior equals the prior, so the gap KL in is also zero. The bound is tight, yet \(z\) carries no information about \(x\): that is posterior collapse.

Homework question: Homework 2, problem 1.4

The KL in problem 1.3 has a closed form, but the reconstruction term generally does not. Construct a Monte Carlo estimator of the reconstruction gradient with respect to \(\phi\) using the reparameterization trick. Give the deterministic map of \(\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\), justify that it produces the desired Gaussian, and state the noise-based reconstruction objective together with its chain-rule gradient.

Homework answer: Homework 2, problem 1.4

Draw \(\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\) independent of \(\phi\), and set \[z=g_\phi(\varepsilon;x,u)=\mu_q(x,u)+\sigma_q(x,u)\odot\varepsilon,\] where \(\sigma_q=\exp(\frac12\log\sigma_q^2)\) if the encoder emits a log-variance. Affine transformation of a standard normal yields \(z\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_q,\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_q^2))=q_\phi(z\mid x,u)\).

A single-sample reconstruction objective (conditioning on \(u\) as in the CVAE) is \[\widehat{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{rec}}(\phi) =\log p_\theta\left(x\mid g_\phi(\varepsilon;x,u), u\right).\] The chain rule differentiates through the sample, \[\nabla_\phi\widehat{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{rec}} =\frac{\partial\log p_\theta}{\partial z} \left( \frac{\partial\mu_q}{\partial\phi} +\varepsilon\odot\frac{\partial\sigma_q}{\partial\phi} \right).\] The estimator is unbiased for \(\nabla_\phi\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z,u)]\) because \(\varepsilon\) does not depend on \(\phi\); the gradient never passes through a score \(\nabla_\phi\log q_\phi\).
