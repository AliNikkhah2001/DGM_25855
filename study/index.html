Block 3. Hierarchical VAE

Theory problem 2. There is no notebook section. Read Tomczak §5.5.2.1 first. The model is p(z2) p(z1 | z2) p(x | z1), with x independent of z2 given z1, and q(z2 | x) q(z1 | z2, x).

  1. 2.1. Start from the KL between the approximate and true joint posteriors, as on Lecture 4 slides 38–39. The ELBO splits into a reconstruction, a KL on z2, and an expected KL on z1 given z2. The inner KL is averaged under q(z2 | x).
  2. 2.2. The hierarchical ELBO is at most the one-layer ELBO on z1. Equality holds when q(z2 | z1, x) equals p(z2 | z1). An auxiliary r(z2 | z1) lower-bounds log p(z1), and the bound is tight when r is that true conditional.

  1. 4.1. Invert the affine coupling with exp(−s). The neural nets s and t never enter the determinant, because the Jacobian stays triangular.
  2. 3.1. Put the Lecture 5 change-of-variables formula into the KL on Lecture 4, slide 78. The prior is the pushforward of N(0, I) through f.
  3. 3.2. The u-space and z-space models have the same marginal likelihood, and the KLs match when the variational densities are exact images of each other. Under a diagonal-Gaussian restriction, f(u1, u2) = (u1, u2 + u1²) takes N(0, I) outside that family, so the restricted problems differ. Tomczak §5.4.1.8.
  4. 5. Glow uses the same W at every pixel. Invertible if and only if det W ≠ 0. The full log-determinant is HW log|det W|. With W = P L (U + diag(s)), that sum collapses to HW times the sum of log|si|.

Block 5. Dequantization

Theory 4.2–4.4, after 4.1. A discrete pixel owns the bin x + [0, 1)^D. Its probability is the integral of the density over that bin, not the density itself. A dequantizer q(u | x) on the bin gives a lower bound by the same Jensen step as Lecture 4, slides 34–35. The gap is a KL inside the bin, so the bound is tight only when q matches the model density renormalized on the bin. Uniform noise is tight only when the density is flat on the bin. A conditional flow can match a non-uniform bin.
